# Beyond Talent: How Grit and Growth Mindset Shape Success
## Slide Content

---

**Slide 1 — Title**
Beyond Talent: How Grit and Growth Mindset Shape Success

**Slide 2 — The Hook**
Two students. Same intelligence. Same grades.
Six months later — completely different outcomes.
What happened in between?

**Slide 3 — The Central Question**
Is success mostly about how talented you are when you start —
or about what you do when things get hard?

**Slide 4 — The Talent Myth**
"He's just naturally talented."
"She's just really smart."
"Born with it."
Talent is real — but it doesn't explain everything.

**Slide 5 — Angela Duckworth**
Seventh-grade math teacher, New York City
Noticed: strongest performers ≠ highest IQ
Question: If intelligence doesn't fully explain achievement — what does?

**Slide 6 — The Research**
West Point cadets
National Spelling Bee competitors
Teachers in under-resourced schools
Rookie salespeople
Same question across every group: who keeps going, and who quits?

**Slide 7 — Grit (Book Cover)**
*Grit: The Power of Passion and Perseverance* — Angela Duckworth
[Book cover image]

**Slide 8 — What Grit Is Not**
Not working hard for one day
Not constant motivation
Not pretending failure doesn't hurt

**Slide 9 — Defining Grit**
Passion and perseverance toward long-term goals
Can you keep going after the motivation disappears?

**Slide 10 — The Marathon Metaphor**
"Life is a marathon, not a sprint." — Angela Duckworth
Sprint: rewards speed
Marathon: rewards staying in it

**Slide 11 — Can Grit Be Taught?**
Duckworth's own answer: no simple formula
"Never give up" is not a strategy

**Slide 12 — Carol Dweck**
A different psychologist. A different idea.
Growth mindset

**Slide 13 — Mindset (Book Cover)**
*Mindset: The New Psychology of Success* — Carol Dweck
[Book cover image]

**Slide 14 — Fixed Mindset**
Ability is fixed
"I'm either good at this or I'm not"
Failure = a verdict on identity

**Slide 15 — Growth Mindset**
Ability develops through practice, strategy, feedback, time
Failure = "I haven't learned this yet"

**Slide 16 — The Shift**
Hard problems → less threatening
Mistakes → data
Feedback → useful
Effort → meaningful

**Slide 17 — Four Practical Shifts**
"I can't do this" → "I can't do this yet"
"Why am I bad at this?" → "What did this teach me?"
Don't confuse effort with improvement
Choose something worth the discomfort

**Slide 18 — Where They Meet**
Growth mindset = a belief: "I can get better"
Grit = a behavior: "I will keep working"
Belief alone doesn't survive week six

**Slide 19 — The Cycle**
Challenge → Failure → Reflection → Learning → Improvement → Confidence → Bigger Challenge

**Slide 20 — The Honest Caveat**
Grit and growth mindset are not a guarantee
Success also depends on opportunity, resources, support, circumstance
"Just work harder" can ignore real barriers

**Slide 21 — Persistence ≠ Repetition**
Repeating a failing strategy harder isn't grit — it's stubbornness
Real perseverance: change the approach, ask for help, get feedback

**Slide 22 — Back to Student A and Student B**
Talent decided where they started
It didn't decide where they ended up

**Slide 23 — Closing Thought**
Talent gives you a starting line
Growth mindset tells you the finish line can move
Grit is what gets you there anyway
